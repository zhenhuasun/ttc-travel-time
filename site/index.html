How it works

  1. 1. Pick a start An address, a station, or your location: the map colors around it.
  2. 2. Click a destination You get the duration and the itinerary: which line to take, transfers, walking.
  3. 3. Compare The 15- and 30-minute curves show what is really close, by tram, subway, or bus.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the travel times come from?

From the official scheduled timetables of each network (TaM (Montpellier), TBM (Bordeaux), TCL (Lyon), Tisséo (Toulouse), RTM (Marseille), Ilévia (Lille), Naolib (Nantes), CTS (Strasbourg), STAR (Rennes), M réso (Grenoble), Lignes d'Azur (Nice), Irigo (Angers), Fil Bleu (Tours), Divia (Dijon), Citura (Reims), SETRAM (Le Mans), Bibus (Brest), T2C (Clermont-Ferrand), STAS (Saint-Étienne), Astuce (Rouen), Ginko (Besançon), TAO (Orléans), STM et REM (Montréal), Île-de-France Mobilités (Paris), STIB (Bruxelles), TEC (Liège), TTC (Toronto), TEC (Charleroi)), published as open data in GTFS format. They cover an ordinary weekday, between 7 a.m. and 8 p.m.

Are the displayed times reliable?

These are "on paper" averages: walk to the stop, waiting time equal to half the interval between two services, scheduled time between stops, and transfers. No real-time data or disruptions.

Are buses included?

Yes, optionally on each map. By default, only trams, subways and guided transit are shown, to highlight the backbone of the network.

My city isn't there — why?

It needs a tram or subway network with timetables published as open data. New cities are added over time: you can suggest one on GitHub, and each new city is announced on X and LinkedIn.

Who made this site?

The idea comes from NYC Transit Time Cartogram by Anthony Castrio, later adapted to Paris by Jules Grandin (C'est encore loin ?). These maps are made by Camille Roux, developer and co-founder of Human Coders in Montpellier: see his other projects and his weekly tech watch. The code is open on GitHub.
